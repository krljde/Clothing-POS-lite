# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary users are small clothing resale shop owners and admins who run discounted SHEIN order operations from a phone or laptop. They manage voucher-bearing accounts, customer order batches, checkout costs, refunds, tracking, ad spend, and profit.

Secondary users are mobile bookers working through `booker.html`. They claim account cards, open customer cart links, mark checkout outcomes, and surrender account login details back to the owner after ordering.

Both groups use the product in an operational context where speed, low ambiguity, and confidence matter more than brand storytelling.

## Product Purpose

SHEIN POS Lite tracks the end-to-end resale workflow for voucher-based account checkouts. It exists to keep account inventory, customer orders, fulfillment handoffs, and profit calculations accurate without a heavyweight back-office system.

Success means the owner can quickly see account availability, order status, ad spend, and real profit, while bookers can complete fulfillment work from a standalone mobile portal without needing access to the main POS.

## Brand Personality

Fast, calm, practical.

The product should feel lightweight, trustworthy, and focused. It can carry a small boutique warmth, but the interface should stay task-first: clear labels, predictable controls, readable financial data, and mobile-first workflows.

## Anti-references

Do not make this feel like a decorative ecommerce storefront, a public marketing landing page, or a flashy SaaS dashboard. Avoid visual choices that slow down repeated data entry: oversized hero sections, ornamental cards, gratuitous animation, low-contrast muted text, and overbuilt navigation.

Avoid turning the booker portal into a consumer shopping experience. It is a work queue and fulfillment checklist, not a catalog.

## Design Principles

1. Speed over spectacle: every screen should help users enter, inspect, or approve operational data quickly.
2. Trust the numbers: profit, costs, refunds, account status, and ad spend need strong hierarchy and consistent formatting.
3. Mobile work is real work: booker flows must remain standalone, touch-friendly, and complete without the main POS shell.
4. Preserve familiar affordances: standard buttons, forms, modals, tabs, and status badges should behave predictably.
5. Show workflow state plainly: users should always know what is open, claimed, fulfilled, failed, surrendered, approved, or blocked.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target practical WCAG AA basics: readable contrast, clear focus states, keyboard-safe modals and forms, accessible labels for icon-only controls, and touch targets sized for mobile use.

Motion should be brief and state-driven, with reduced-motion-safe behavior. Color should never be the only indicator of status; pair semantic color with text labels or icons where status matters.
